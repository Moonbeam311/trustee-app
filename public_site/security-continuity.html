Security and continuity

Access, authority, history, and preservation belong together.

Hindsfoot's verified architecture emphasizes controlled access and traceable action while recognizing that secure operation depends on the complete environment.

Authenticated access

Institutional workspaces require an authenticated session; the public presentation layer exposes no protected record.

Role-aware operation

Available actions remain governed by established roles and permissions rather than authentication alone.

Firm-scoped records

Institutional records and retrieval paths preserve firm context and isolation.

Governed event histories

Important transitions retain attributable, ordered evidence instead of silently overwriting the only history.

Confirmation before consequence

Required information and explicit review stand between a draft proposal and later lifecycle action.

Continuity orientation

Records, routes, and operational context are designed to remain understandable through transition and recovery.

Security is an operating responsibility.

Security, privacy, resilience, and compliance depend on configuration, hosting, deployment, identity administration, updates, operator practices, data governance, and appropriate professional review. Architectural safeguards do not create an unconditional security or compliance guarantee.

Genealogical information requires care.

Living-person details, relationship assertions, photographs, source records, and sensitive family context require role-aware access, deliberate handling, and appropriate retention and disclosure practices.
